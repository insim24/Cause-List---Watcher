10-03-2023
1. The instant petition initially came up for consideration on 12.1.2023
on which date notice came to be issued to the respondents by the court
and same was accepted by Mr. T. M. Shamsi, DSGI on behalf of
respondents 1 and 2. Notice on the said date was also waived by the
counsel present for respondent 8 and appearing counsel on behalf of
respondents came to be provided an opportunity to file response to the
petition by or before the next date while summoning the rest of the
respondents and the matter was directed to be listed for consideration
on 9.3.2023. The record of the WP (C) 2248/2022 was also
summoned by this court.
2. Upon coming up the matter for consideration today, record reveals
that the appearing counsel for the respondents 1 and 2, and 8, have not

WP (C) 33/2023 Page 2 of 6
filed any reply in compliance to order dated 12.1.2023. Mr. Lone
appearing counsel for the petitioners would contend that in view of
the failure of respondents to file reply, the application for interim
relief requires to be considered in view of the emergent nature of
controversy involved in the writ petition. Mr. Shamsi, appearing
counsel for the respondents 1 and 2, however, would oppose the
consideration of the application for interim relief primarily and
fundamentally on the ground that the petitioners prior to the filing of
the instant petition had filed WP (C) 2248/2022 supra having sought
therein same reliefs as are being sought in the instant petition.
Heard learned counsel for the parties and perused of the record.
3. Perusal of the record tends to show that in respect of the petitioner
educational institution, the respondent 2/Defence Estates Officer had
addressed communication no. B-38/20/Vol-1/FMS-ID-1997 dated
13.9.2022, impugned in the instant petition, to the respondent 3,
Divisional Commissioner Kashmir besides seeking therein a report
regarding extension of lease and eviction of illegal occupant namely
Mrs. Grace Paljor on the premise that the petitioner educational
institution is located within the cantonment limits providing that the
said school is being run illegally on the defence land without
permission from the Ministry of Defence and that as such the office is
in the process of initiating eviction proceedings.
4. Perusal of the communication dated 13.9.2022, supra, also reveals
that the Defence Estates Officer, respondent 2, had also requested
Divisional Commissioner, Kashmir, respondent 3, to look into the
matter of recognition of the school by the Education Department as
there were several shortcomings with the respect to the non-

WP (C) 33/2023 Page 3 of 6
compliance of the NOCs/permissions from the concerned line
departments and most importantly there being no consent of the
Ministry of Defence being the owner of the land thus recommending
that the recognition/affiliation of the petitioner educational institution
be cancelled at the earliest if deemed fit.
5. Perusal of the record would further reveal that in furtherance of letter
dated 13.9.2022, the respondent 3 Divisional Commissioner in turn
addressed communication dated 21.9.2022 to Director, School
Education, Kashmir directing him to take necessary action by way of
closing the school after proper accommodation of the students in
nearby school, with a further direction that if the management of the
school creates law and order issue and instigates parents, they be
booked under relevant section of law. An action taken report was also
sought by the respondent 3 Divisional Commissioner in this behalf.
Perusal of the record further tends to show that letter dated 21.9.2022
was followed by a communication dated 1.10.2022 addressed by Joint
Director School, Directorate of School Education, Kashmir to Chief
Education Officer, Srinagar, reiterating therein instructions conveyed
by respondent 3 Divisional Commissioner to the Director School
Education in his letter dated 21.9.2022. The said communication is
followed by an eviction notice dated 30.12.2022 issued by respondent
Tehsildar South Srinagar addressed to the Principal of the petitioner
educational institution as it emerges from the record communicating
therein that the physical custody of the school has been taken over as
per the directions of respondent 3 and further calling upon the
Principal of the petitioner educational institution to take out all items

WP (C) 33/2023 Page 4 of 6
including furniture within seven days failing which action under
relevant provisions of the law shall follow and items will be seized.
6. In view of the aforesaid process initiated by the respondent 3
Divisional Commissioner, Kashmir, purportedly pursuant to the
communication of respondent 2 Defence Estates Officer dated
13.9.2022, the school in question is said to have been closed by the
respondent Tehsildar and students studying in the said school
accommodated in some other nearby school.
7. Perusal of the record suggests indisputably that the land wherein the
school is existing belongs to respondents 1 and 2. The respondent 2
Defence Estates Officer in terms of communication dated 13.9.2022
required the respondent 3 Divisional Commissioner Kashmir to look
into the issuance of recognition of the said school by the Education
Department inasmuch as non-compliance of NOCs/permission from
the concerned departments as also for recommending follow-up action
by the Divisional Commissioner for cancellation of
recognition/affiliation of the school. The respondent 2 Defence
Estates Officer did not in terms of the communication dated 13.9.2022
either make any request or recommendation to the Divisional
Commissioner for closure of the school. The Divisional
Commissioner as also the school education authorities including the
Tehsildar seemingly have undertaken the said exercise of closure of
the school and eviction of students studying therein, of their own
without any sanction of law or authority inasmuch as ex facie has been
undertaken without any competence and jurisdiction.
8. It is pertinent to note that during the course of submissions made by
counsel for the petitioners, a reference and heavy reliance was placed

WP (C) 33/2023 Page 5 of 6
on the policy dated June 2021 issued by Government of India
regarding use of defence land inside cantonments by the educational
institutions which at paras 2 and 3 provide as under:
2. Govt. of India, Ministry of Defence has taken note of the fact that most
of these educational institutions are running since long, imparting formal
or vocational education to the students coming from Cantonments as well
as adjoining areas. Keeping in view the public interest involved. it has
been decided to regulate the sites occupied by such educational
institutions in accordance with a policy, the details of which are provided
hereunder.
3. This policy is applicable only to such educational institutions which are
recognized by any of the Central or State Govt Authority I Education
Board I UGC I AICTE I any oth~r Statutory Authority empowered to
grant such recognition. Only existing educational institutions shall be
covered under this policy. No new site shall be offered on lease under the
terms of this policy. However, Govt. educational inslitutions, Cantonment
Board Schools and educational institutions run by any or the three
services i.e. the Army, the Navy and the Air Force, will continue to be
governed by the extant instructions related to them.
A bare perusal of the aforesaid policy would manifestly
demonstrate that the Government of India had not been
oblivious to the fact that certain educational institutions are
being run and operated inside cantonments and as such the
policy supra provided for regularization of the sites occupied
by such institutions in accordance with the policy.
9. In presence of the aforesaid policy as well, the respondent 3
Divisional Commissioner, Kashmir, and other respondents had no
occasion to close down the petitioner institution and to dislodge the
students studying therein in any other educational institution.
10. Having regard to the aforesaid facts and circumstances a case for grant
of indulgence and interim relief is made out at this stage in favour of
the petitioner. In view of the fact that petitioner educational institution

WP (C) 33/2023 Page 6 of 6
has been closed down, this court cannot lose sight of the fact that the
academic session has recently commenced in the Valley therefore in
the larger interest of the students who have been studying in the
petitioner educational institution, it is deemed appropriate to direct the
desealing of the petitioner educational institution for permitting the
students to pursue their studies therein. Accordingly, the respondent
Divisional Commissioner is directed to forthwith ensure the desealing
of the petitioner educational institution and allow it to run and operate
without any interference or impediment.
11. Modification, vacation, alteration on motion.
12. List on 28.4.2023.
13. Meanwhile, respondents may file objections/reply.
