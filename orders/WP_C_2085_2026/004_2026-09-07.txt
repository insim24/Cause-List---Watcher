1. The petitioner is an accused in FIR No. 337/2025, registered for the
commission of offences under Section 8/23-B of the Immigration
and Foreigners Act, 2025 (hereinafter referred to as “the Act” or “the
Special Act”). The prosecution case, in essence, is that the petitioner
failed to report, in the prescribed Form-C (Form-III), the stay of a
foreign national at his homestay, as required under the Rules framed
under the Act. The alleged omission is stated to have attracted the
aforesaid penal provisions, culminating in the presentation of the
charge-sheet dated 29.12.2025, which is presently pending
consideration before the Court of Chief Judicial Magistrate, Budgam.

2. The case set up by the petitioner is that, by virtue of Section 25 of the
Act, the offences alleged against him are compoundable and that the
competent authority, namely respondent No. 2-Foreigners Regional
Registration Officer (FRRO), Amritsaris empowered to compound
the specified offence either before or after the institution of
prosecution. It is pleaded that the petitioner had approached
respondent No. 4- Foreigners Registration Officer, Budgam by way
of an appropriate representation/application seeking compounding of
the offence. However, no decision thereon was taken, compelling the
petitioner to invoke the writ jurisdiction of this Court.
3. During the course of hearing, ld. counsel for the petitioner submitted
that the grievance of the petitioner lies in a narrow compass. It was
submitted that since the petitioner has already approached the
competent authority seeking compounding of the offence, the
petitioner would be satisfied if the present petition is disposed of
with a direction to the concerned authority to consider and decide his
request for compounding in accordance with law.
4. Per contra, learned counsel for the respondents opposed the petition
and submitted that, although the offences in question are
compoundable, the remedy available to the petitioner is to approach
the Trial Court for compounding of the offence in accordance with
law. It was further contended that even if, as a matter of procedure,
an application for compounding is required to be moved by the
complainant or the competent authority, the petitioner is not
remediless. According to the respondents, the petitioner may avail
himself of the remedy of plea bargaining as contemplated under

Chapter XXI-A (Sections 265-A to 265-L) of the Code of Criminal
Procedure, 1973. It was, accordingly, argued that an equally
efficacious alternative remedy is available to the petitioner and,
therefore, invocation of the extraordinary writ jurisdiction of this
Court is unwarranted.
5. Having heard learned counsel for the parties and considered the
material on record, it emerges that, as on date, the petitioner is facing
prosecution pursuant to the charge-sheet presented for the
commission of offences under Sections 8/23-B of the Act, which is
presently pending before the Court of Chief Judicial Magistrate,
Budgam. It is not in dispute that the offence in question is
compoundable under the Act. The petitioner also appears to have
approached the Senior Superintendent of Police, Budgam the
Investigating Agency seeking compounding of the offence. In
response thereto, vide communication dated 29.04.2026, the
petitioner was advised to approach the Trial Court for appropriate
directions with regard to compounding of the offence in accordance
with the procedure prescribed under Section 25 of the Act, including
the requirement of obtaining approval of the Government of India,
wherever so required.
6. The petitioner, in essence, seeks issuance of a writ of mandamus
commanding respondents Nos. 1 to 4 to process his case for
compounding of the offence. The question that, therefore, arises for
consideration is whether such a mandamus can be issued at the
instance of the petitioner. The answer has necessarily to be in the
negative, for the respondents cannot be said to be under a
WP(C) No. 2085/2026 Page 3 of 7

corresponding statutory obligation to compound the offence merely
at the asking of the petitioner. The counsel for the petitioner,
however, invited the attention of this Court to an order passed by the
Hon’ble High Court of Kerala in WP(Crl.) No. 1405/2025, whereby
the writ petition therein came to be disposed of with a direction
permitting/requiring the petitioner to submit an application
containing the necessary particulars for compounding of the offence.
7. This Court has heard learned counsel for the petitioner on the
aforesaid aspect and has also carefully gone through the judgment
cited at the Bar. Since the offence for which the petitioner is being
prosecuted is an offence under a special enactment, the procedure
governing its investigation, inquiry and trial has to be understood in
the light of Section 4(2) of the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita,
2023 (“BNSS”). Section 4(2) of the BNSS, in substance, provides
that offences under any law other than the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita,
2023 (“BNS”) are to be investigated, inquired into, tried and
otherwise dealt with in accordance with the provisions of the BNSS,
subject, however, to any enactment for the time being in force
regulating the manner or place of investigating, inquiring into, trying
or otherwise dealing with such offences. Consequently, where the
Special Act prescribes a particular procedure, such procedure would
govern; and in matters on which the Special Act is silent, the
provisions of the BNSS would apply.
8. Section 25 of the Special Act makes a specific provision for
compounding of certain offences, including offences punishable
under Sections 21, 23 and 24 of the Act. The provision enables the
WP(C) No. 2085/2026 Page 4 of 7

specified offences to be compounded, either before the institution of
prosecution or during the course of trial, by such officer or authority
as may be duly authorised in that behalf. Sub-section (3) further
provides that every officer or authority referred to in sub-section (1)
shall exercise the power of compounding subject to the direction,
control and supervision of the Central Government.
9. The statutory scheme further contemplates that where an offence is
compounded after institution of prosecution or during the course of
trial, the factum of such compounding is required to be brought by
the competent authority to the notice of the Court before which the
prosecution or trial is pending. Upon such intimation being given, the
individual, company or organisation, or the officer or employee
thereof, as the case may be, in relation to whom the offence has been
compounded, is to be discharged in accordance with law. Thus,
Section 25 envisages a statutory mechanism for compounding of
specified offences by the authority duly empowered in that behalf,
subject to the conditions and supervisory framework contemplated
by the Act.
10. In the present case, no application seeking compounding of the
offence appears to have been moved by the petitioner before the
learned Trial Court, notwithstanding the fact that the prosecution is
already pending before it. Instead, the petitioner has invoked the
extraordinary writ jurisdiction of this Court seeking a direction to the
respondents to process his request for compounding. The mere fact
that an offence is compoundable does not, by itself, confer upon an
accused an indefeasible right to have the offence compounded.
WP(C) No. 2085/2026 Page 5 of 7

Compounding has to take place in the manner contemplated by the
statute and through the authority empowered by law to exercise such
power.
11. At the same time, there can be no quarrel with the proposition that
where the State or an authority constituted by it is vested with the
statutory power to consider compounding of an offence, such power
is required to be exercised fairly, reasonably and in accordance with
the procedure prescribed by law. This, however, would not entitle the
petitioner, in the facts of the present case, to seek a writ commanding
the respondents to compound the offence or to process the matter in a
particular manner dehors the statutory procedure.
12. Once the charge-sheet has been presented and the criminal
proceedings are pending before the competent Trial Court, it would
be appropriate for the petitioner to approach the said Court with an
application seeking such relief as may be permissible under law.
Upon such an application being moved, it shall be open to the Trial
Court, having regard to the statutory scheme under Section 25 of the
Act, to deal with the same in accordance with law and, where
necessary, call upon the competent compounding authority to take
such steps as are contemplated under the Act. In these circumstances,
no case for exercise of the extraordinary writ jurisdiction of this
Court is made out.
13. For the foregoing reasons, this Court finds no merit in the present
petition. The same is, accordingly, dismissed.
14. It is, however, made clear that dismissal of the present petition shall
not preclude the petitioner from moving an appropriate application
WP(C) No. 2085/2026 Page 6 of 7

before the Trial Court seeking compounding of the offence. In the
event such an application is filed, the Trial Court shall consider and
deal with the same, in accordance with law, having regard to the
provisions of Section 25 of the Act and without being influenced by
the dismissal of the present writ petition.
